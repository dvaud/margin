### Changes that can alter your results

- **Reader 6 reads PDFs more cleanly: text a figure hides, publishing details at the foot of the first page, the
  title and running heads.** Some publishers (MDPI among them) make a figure by showing a window onto a whole draft
  page; reader 5 read the hidden rest of that page too, as dozens of one-line "figures" that broke the paragraphs
  around them in pieces. Reader 6 reads only what the figure shows, so it is one figure with its caption and the
  paragraphs are whole. The e-mail addresses, the DOI and the dates ("Received …", "Available online …") that Elsevier
  and others print at the foot of the first page, after the introduction has begun, are set aside as front matter
  instead of read as text, as is a paragraph that is only a link or a DOI. A journal's name printed larger than the
  title is no longer taken for the title when the name begins the running head (as at Elsevier), and a title over two
  lines is kept whole. A running head with the page number at the other end of its line is found on every page, not
  every other one. So it no longer reads as a heading that cuts a reference list or the end notes in two: a reference
  list that runs over a page is now left out of the passages as a whole, and a document can count noticeably fewer
  words (one Elsevier paper went from 10,605 to 9,003). A byline or a date before the text, as in a newspaper article
  ("By …", "May 8, 2012"), is now set aside with the front matter too. Word files, web pages and text files read as
  before, except one in two languages (below). **Can change results, for documents you move to it.** New documents are read by reader 6; PDFs already in a
  project keep their reading until you choose *Read with the new reader*.

### Not yet done

- A journal's name on a cover page (Taylor & Francis) can still be taken for the title, and a title set beside a
  column of other text can still come out in pieces.
- An appendix after the end notes is left out with them when its heading is not recognised ("Appendix B.
  Description …"); in one Elsevier paper this now leaves out the appendix's second page as well as its first.
